## What is verified (the three big results)

1. **The audio core is decrypted.** The OP-1 encrypts part of its firmware (L1) with an XTEA key in
   OTP page 0xD0, the same on every unit. We did not need to extract it from your device: the public
   reference dump (Tolsi/op1dumps) is valid for all units. Our tool verified it against hundekraft's
   known plaintext/ciphertext pairs, then decrypted the whole L1. Three independent checks confirm
   the result: the crt0 sets the stack to the documented value, the audio callback reads the cycle
   counter, and the LFO dispatch matches the research notes. Entropy dropped from 7.99 (encrypted) to
   6.78 (plain code). Details in `docs/l1-map.md`.

2. **op1emu boots our firmware.** We built op1emu (an MIT BF52x emulator), provisioned a NAND image
   from your `op1_246.op1`, produced a decrypted LDR, and booted it headless. It reaches the OP-1
   tape screen. This is now the development loop: no flash-and-photograph cycles needed for most work.
   Details in `docs/emulator.md`.

3. **Our audio callback runs.** coop-fw repoints the audio-callback seam (an instruction pair at
   `0x0191AB52` that installs the stock callback `0xffa04804`) to our own function, which calls the
   stock one and times it. The coop image boots normally in op1emu. A negative control (a callback
   that spins instead of forwarding) hangs the firmware, proving our code executes every audio block.
   Details in `docs/phase2.md`.

## A key architectural finding

The audio callback lives in the encrypted L1, but **most of the audio graph is plain, unencrypted
SDRAM** (the per-stage DSP functions). Only the orchestration and the tightest inner kernels (the
voice loop, the tape resampler, the mix meters, two helpers) are in L1. This means coop-fw does not
have to rewrite the whole core in L1: it can drive the existing SDRAM DSP from its own callback and
replace only the stages it must (the stereo buses, the master suite). This materially lowers the risk
and effort of Phase 2. The graph's block order is mapped in `docs/audio-graph.md`, with the function
labels in `symbols/sdram.sym` and `symbols/l1.sym`.

## The dumper (now optional)

Phase 1's original plan was an on-device dumper. It works (and is committed), but it is no longer
needed for the gate, because the public OTP made the whole extraction unnecessary. It remains as an
optional device-vs-emulator cross-check. The one caution worth remembering from it: never touch MDMA0
(`0xffc00f00`), which is the firmware's display engine — doing so hangs the device, which is what the
first flash did.
